I'm less interested in technology for its own sake than in what it can do for people who are usually overlooked.

I'm a first-generation college student studying Computer Science and Political Science at the University of Massachusetts Boston. I came to the United States from Guatemala as a child, and the road here ran through a regional technical high school and a plumbing trade — and a lot of catching up — before it ran through a research lab.

That route shapes how I work. I build things that are technically serious and put them in service of something human: patients, immigrants, families navigating systems that were never built for them. I lead a research team, I organize communities, and I try to keep both hands on the same problem.

I'm working toward a future that bridges engineering, law, and public service — and a long-term commitment to the communities I come from, in the United States and in Latin America.
